In this section we’ll go over more observations, with a focus on common play patterns and their consequences for the Seton’s meta.

## Area threatened by Navy

Navy is by far the most important part of Seton’s combat, but what can you even kill with T1/T2/T3 navy?

## Area Threatened by Frigs

Not much is killable by frigs, only like 2 mexes on each side. Aeon frigs can kill a few more mexes than the other factions, but still not that many, and to kill them you need to put them at exactly the right spots, which is quite finicky.

While a frig rush can still be very strong, a navy win with just frigs is more or less irrelevant if you can’t maintain it until you can deal more damage with higher tech navy.

## Area Threatened by Destros

As expected, there are very large faction differences when it comes to destros. Sera in particular gets shafted. Their 60 range laser destros can barely hit anything of relevance due to the high cliff faces everywhere. The Mid bases in particular are completely untouchable by Sera destros. Even Sera destros can completely clean the entire causeway and at least some parts of the Beach and Rock core mexes though.

UEF and Aeon have a much easier time in that they can kill all of the core mexes of Rock and Beach with their destros, as well as all of the forward mexes in Mid and most of the Islands.
Cybran Destros with their 85 range cover the most ground by far and are the only ones that can at least attempt to kill the Mid bases. In practice this is still hard to do, even with them, as the cliffs protecting the Mid base results in many shots being blocked for free, and hence an easy cover for T2 arty/float spam by the enemy Mid player.
Cybran destros can of course also walk on land directly, but this is not factored in here. While this can be a good move, it relies on complete air control for them to not immediately die to a couple gunships.
Importantly, a couple shields and T2 arty are enough to zone out even a dozen destroyers relatively easily. Unless you can kill their main base before that, you will have to make battleships to break them.
While TML cruisers, the UEF and Sera ones, can also be used to pressure enemy bases with their 128 range missiles (see next section), they will only be able to destroy a base with a lot of assistance. Due to the high amount of eco on Seton’s it’s relatively trivial to build as many TMDs as you need everywhere, and a good opponent will do exactly that. TML cruisers can still do a lot of damage when used correctly, but to finish a base off, you will need destros or battleships more often than not.

## Area Threatened by Battleships

A navy win during the T3 stage automatically results in two out of four bases being completely destroyed via battleships. Battleships have no problem shooting over the cliff faces protecting Mid that even the Cybran destros struggled with. Notably, the Mid base will get destroyed regardless of which side the navy loss occurs on. As such you should think twice about putting extended post-T3 infrastructure (like mass fabs, nukes, etc) there if you are playing as Mid.

Now that T2 arties have less range than battleships (115 vs 128), no amount of static defense will be able to defend against multiple of them for a significant amount of time.

## Firebase Creeping in Mid

If you have played a couple games on setons Mid, the following has probably happened to you: The reclaim is gone, unit production has stopped, both sides have at least one T2 PD up. The causeway is stable.
Yet, for some unholy reason, your opponent didn’t get the memo. They start spamming more T2 PD, shields, and/or T2 arty, ravagers and MMLs, putting more and more pressure onto your position.  While there is sometimes a short window where you can overrun such an enemy position with ACU upgrades and units alone, and you should do so if you can, what do you do when you miss this window?  What choice do you have? You do the same, hunker down behind PD and shields, and suddenly both of you have 20k mass in static defense on the causeway, fighting over basically nothing, but you can’t stop either as that would mean losing all that invested mass.
Even if you win the firebase fight, you only get a bit of reclaim and can deny a single mex of your enemy. A couple minutes later the causeway gets annihilated by T3 navy, and you realize that you are now behind on eco and spent the last 20 minutes contributing nothing to the game.
There must be a better way..

## How to Counter Firebase Creeping - T2 Navy

And there is! It’s called a tactical retreat. The plan is the following: Surrender a bit of territory to slow down your opponent’s firebase creep enough that you can make T2 navy to kill or bypass it entirely.
At the outset a normal situation looks something like this:

If your enemy starts to T2 PD creep you, you can, and should, give up on your mex in the front and build a couple T2 PDs and a shield slightly further back.

Then you reclaim the mexes and PDs up front. Now your enemy needs to build enough T2 PDs slightly further forward to pressure you away from your new situation, where you can just repeat

the process.
This is micro intensive for both you and your opponent, but most importantly it’s much more costly for them than it is for you. While this is happening, you put your extra money into quickly going for T2 navy. Once done, you use the navy to kill the firebase if you must, or bypass it entirely to just kill all their mexes.

Try to get your beach/rock to assist you with this, but even if they don’t, this is still the correct response.
If your enemy is pressuring you with ravagers/T2 arties you do more or less the same. The main difference is that you want more shields for stalling and that you retreat later, but further.

Do not make any T2 arty or ravagers yourself. Put it all into T2 navy instead.
Notably T2 arty and ravagers are insanely expensive, about 2k mass each, and you need half a dozen of them to break through even a couple t2 shields. Coincidentally, 2k mass is also what a destroyer costs, so while your enemy is making 5 t2 arties to kill a single one of your mexes, you make 5 t2 destros and just kill all of their mexes in response.

## When Stalling into T2 Navy Doesn’t Work

Giving up territory to stall the enemy is only viable if you have safe territory to retreat to. If the early game went badly for you, and your opponent is able to make a firebase in T2 arty range of

your main base you can’t retreat from there. In fact, you should try to prevent a firebase being built there at basically any cost, including suiciding your ACU to draw with them. Threat levels of a firebase in Mid look roughly like this:
 Yes, all Mid firebases will at some point be destroyed by T3 navy, if the game goes long enough. Yet, if you let your enemy establish a firebase in T2 arty range of your Mid base, the red part highlighted above, it’s likely that you will die, your Mid base will fall and then you’ll have lost the game before anyone on your team even has a T3 naval yard.

A firebase that close to your core Mid base is an all hands on deck situation and, against a player with equal skill, you’ll need your allies to help you. The only real answer is to not let them advance that far in the first place, i.e. to play better in the next game.
The mirror of this situation, you having the option to make a firebase in T2 arty range of their Mid base, is the only time when you might want to PD/ravager/arty creep yourself. Doing so is a high risk high reward move. If it works, you probably win the game. If not, you’ll probably just die.

## Other (Usually Incorrect) Responses

In the interest of being comprehensive, let’s quickly go over other seemingly sensible responses and why they usually don’t work.

## Ignoring the Creep to Just Eco

If the firebase creep is actually threatening, you will die.

## Telling Beach/Rock to Deal with It

Technically, this can work, especially if your allies already have a significant amount of T2 navy. If they do have that, they are currently either preparing to, or already in the process of, attacking their navy counterpart, hence you should probably help them out too and the best way to do that is to make T2 navy of your own.

## MMLs

Spamming MMls can be a useful delay tactic, especially if you are cybran, and the opponent is non-aeon, but against an opponent with equal ressources, you’ll never be able to break the firebase this way.
In contrast to other maps, MMLs also can’t be used for anything else, so in effect spamming MMLs commits the same mistake as making your own firebase.

## T2 Tonks to Overrun the Firebase

If you catch your opponent of guard, spamming a giant army to just walk into the firebase can technically work, but a good opponent will see what you are doing and prepare in time.

## T2 Floaty to Go Around the Firebase

This is more sensible than non-floaty tanks, but even if you can walk around the firebase, you can’t directly pressure the enemy’s mid base with it, as if you do, your tanks will likely just end up as a mass donation.  As such, walking around the Mid base to assist one of your allies’ fights for the pond is usually a better choice, but if you are doing that, you may as well just build T2 navy instead.

## T3 Land / T3 Mobile Arty / T4s

If the firebase creep is actually threatening, you will be dead before you get any of these.

## Going All-In Somewhere Else

This can actually work! Firebase Creeping is a very slow way of attacking, so even if you only lightly stall it, you have multiple minutes before you’ll actually lose your base. You can use this time to deal hopefully game winning damage elsewhere. Examples include T2 drops, T2 bombers, a T3 TML com and/or a T3 TML com drop.
Do realize though that this is more likely than not a true all-in strategy. If you don’t deal enough damage elsewhere to win the game faster than you get creeped out of your own Mid base, you’ll probably just lose the game.

## Summary - Intermediate Guide

Even if you win the navy fight for your pond on T1/T2 navy, you’ll almost always have to make battleships to kill the enemy main bases.
If your Mid opponent tries to PD creep you, slowly retreat and stall them while going T2 navy yourself. Then just ignore the firebase and kill everything behind it.

## Racing to Win

Your team won a pond, the enemy team won a pond. Mid and Rock/Beach are under threat from T3 navy and will be destroyed soon. What now? Trying to win back a lost pond is effectively impossible, so it comes down to leveraging your pond win better than the enemy does theirs. Off to the races it is! 
Notably, winning faster than your enemies is only necessary when both teams win one pond each, as any team that wins both ponds wins the game by default. The only way to win after having lost both ponds is a completed paragon, everything else, even non-paragon game enders, will just die to experimentals, nukes and/or enemy game enders.
We’ll discuss both winning faster and losing slower at the same time, as they mirror each other.

## Stalling a Land Invasion

Once the enemy has a foothold on your side of the map, it’s basically impossible to drive them out again. Once the facs are placed, it’s too easy for them to spend a lot of mass there, too much for you to be able to fight it head on.
Hence what you want to do is prevent your enemy from getting a foothold for as long as possible. In practice this can’t be done indefinitely, but even a couple minutes of additional time can easily be the difference between winning and losing.
One of the best ways to do that is to start a production of T2 float spam far out of reach of the enemy’s battleships as soon as you realize that you are losing.  Importantly, you don’t want to send this float spam into the enemy navy. They’ll just die there, achieving nothing. Make 50-100 T2 float tanks, and keep them in reserve. Once the enemy tries to place factories on your side of the map, send some tanks to clean up all the engies and

factories you can. If done well, you can prevent land fall for quite some time without much mass investment on your side. Eventually, this will fail of course, but again, in this situation you are just stalling so that your allies can hopefully win the game in the meantime.

## Nuke Subs

Seton’s happens to be the perfect map for nuke subs.
First off, they are just convenient to build. Having a lot of navy build power is a must to win a pond, but T3 navy alone can’t finish a game. When you stop building navy, you suddenly have a lot of unused mass and build power that is already next to T3 naval yards.
Nuke subs can nuke every other base from any pond.
Inside of a won pond, they are very hard to scout and even harder to snipe. Being hard to scout is more important than it might seem, as it means your opponents can’t easily judge the number of SMDs they need. Maybe they need only one, maybe they need
Them having very strong, 256 range TMLs that can occasionally be used to snipe unguarded SMDs or other important buildings is just gravy.  Note that you need 40 mass and 5000 energy per second to load a nuke. While the mass shouldn't be a problem, you will need to make two additional T3 pgens per nuke you are loading, which very quickly becomes a lot of pgens. Even if you only make 5 nuke subs, a very reasonable number for late-game Seton’s, that’s 10 additional T3 pgens, and it only goes up from there. Don’t underestimate that energy demand.

## Defending against Nuke Subs

Sometimes you can just make a lot of SMDs, especially if you are making a game ender that will win the game if it finishes. Another useful move you should consider is trying to scout the Nuke Subs and try to torp them specifically. This is especially good if the Navy Player didnt need to build a lot of Cruisers etc. to win his pond.

## Random Notes - Defending against a Novax

Shields! Shields are your only viable option for defending against the Sat, and the key is to scout the sat quickly to give you plenty of time to shield important structures like your mexes and core base.

## Mex Shielding

Shielding your mex is particularly important since when facing the threat of a sat, your mex are likely to be fully capped with storages and t2 mass fabs (either 4 or 8 fabs), making them very vulnerable and volatile. An exposed, unshielded T3 mex with 4 mass storages and 8 t2 mass fabs will take a sat roughly 23 seconds to kill, and that’s including the reload time of the sat. How to shield mex? Obviously you just build a shield next to the mex, although it is slightly nuanced.

As you can see, a basic Cybran T2 shield does not quite cover the entirety of this structure, leaving at least 3 fabs exposed. Upon a fire cycle, a Sat will kill these 3 instantly and start working its way through your shield.

Since Cybran has the weakest T2 shield you want at least 2 basic shields, or simply upgrade your 1 existing shield however many times you like, 1 upgrade is sufficient to cover the whole

structure.
Of course the more shields you add the safer it will be. Just bear in mind the extra resources it will cost you.

If you want to be extra safe it’s worth considering this 4 shield template which for the most part, fits perfectly around your mex/fab structure. Doing so usually results in your opponent thinking

twice about attacking it, forcing them to look for weaker and more vulnerable targets.
Please note that it’s not possible to shield every mex on the map with this 4 shield template, but for the most part you can use at least 3.

Of course a T3 shield is superior to T2, and for some mex you can shield multiple at once. Again consider the resources required for the T3 shield vs T2 shields.
Not only are resources a factor in this decision, but equally so is your APM and time required to shield every single mex with at least 1 to 2 T2 shields. If you spot the Sat early, i.e. still being built, this gives you plenty of time to prepare so I would suggest multiple T2 shields around every mex you have. If you don’t see the Sat until it’s too late, and you see it zooming toward base then you will likely lose mex if you’re it’s target. So your options are to grind out full shielding on every mex, taking note of appropriate engies closest to mex, or simply rush a T3 shield on the nearest group of mexes.

## Air Base Shielding

For every slot on setons, your core base is likely the biggest target for a Sat. However the most critical structure for both teams is going to be the air grid in most cases.

Essentially, you want to shield your entire air grid so that nothing is exposed, especially your pgens. We all know this. This is usually what a setons air grid looks like in the core base of the south air player. Not all the terrain is flat so this is roughly how big the core air base will be.

It’s important not to build a pgen next to every single air fac where it’s possible, for the simple purpose of shielding. Your air grid can be the target of a Sat, but other threats as well, like surprise TML’s, a surprise stealth strat bomb run etc..

## Rock’s Island

Rushing your island/Not losing the island in the first place
As the rock player, it’s crucial for you to secure your island ASAP, which usually means rushing it with a quick transport loaded with only 3 engies.
However, you may be facing an aggressive beach player who might want to steal your island.
From the offset, there are some things to note here: Knowing which players are on the enemy side can tell you a lot. If you are in a high rated game, then there is a good chance your opponent is going to try to steal your island. Of course this is dependent on the individual's play style, and it doesn’t have to be a high rated player, or even in a high rated game for that matter. Anyone with the “know-how”, or confidence will attempt it.
So, your opponent is brazen enough to go for your island, what do you do as the rock player?
Don’t start with a land factory. Instead go 1st air fac, and 2nd air fac a bit after.
In your 1st air fac you typically want 3 engies, 1 scout, and the rest engies, which will go on to their usual attack reclaim orders. When you begin your 2nd air fac, your first unit can either be a transport (rushing island), or counter air units (defending island).

## Recovering a lost island

As the rock player, the island is essentially yours since it is closer to you than any other slot. When someone successfully steals your island, do not panic (easier said than done in practice). You have not lost at this point and you can certainly get it back by making the right moves.
For the first few minutes, continue playing as normal, i.e. reclaiming your trees, upgrading mex, making pgens and making air etc.. You need to also establish your naval factories in the usual locations, as well as potentially to the side of your core base.

Never stop scouting. That means continue scouting the island for possible drops; seeing how much effort is being put into spamming early navy, and also scouting their core base for any other shenanigans or possible counter drops/counter attacks you can make.
How to actually get the island back?
The first way, which is probably not the most effective but can work., is to respond quickly. Assuming your initial drop failed, your engies died on the island and you lost your transport, you should rush another transport and load with as many engies as possible. You can try dropping these on the island where you’ll rush a fac/reclaim the existing fac, or rush a pd, then then fac, and so on.
The second method is the same, except instead of your transport being full of engies, load it with say 1-2 engies and the rest attacking units, say 3-4 tanks and an arty.
The most effective way is to drop your ACU on the island which usually has a 100% success rate of taking back and securing. BUT: Don't drop your unupgraded ACU, the enemy can spam t1 pds quite fast after he's established and your ACU will die very quickly. Either drop a gun (which is the worse option) or a T2 ACU. Overcharge the engies and build pd on your own when youre T2, when youre gun you want to OC the engies and then the pd. And as a gun ACU you want to abuse the max-range so that the ACU doesnt get shot by the T1 PD.
Another effective way of getting your island back is rushing T2 Navy. This is easily possible as long as your enemy isn't building much of T1 Navy. If he does: just build Torp Launcher as protection since they're countering T1 navy very effectively.

## Defending against a Tele Mazor

Telemazor is a somewhat controversial subject in FAF, as we all know. It’s one of those things that you either hate it and never use it, or rarely use it, or you absolutely love it and will use it in any game possible. We know both sets of these players exist.
Your best bet at countering a tele mazor is simply PD.
Either t1 or t2 PD will work. Obviously t1 pd is cheaper to make and less effective due to range compared to t2 PD. Of course t2 PD is more expensive but will offer better coverage of the area you wish to tele proof.
What to Tele Proof?
Mid The mid slots are seldom locations for a tele mazor attempt, but it’s still worth making PD here as the mid player, especially around your SMD. A combination of t1 and t2, or just t1 will suffice.
