# Seton's: the beginner guide

By CheeseBerry.

Seton's has been played a ton over the years, with a well developed meta.
When starting to play Seton’s, you only really need to know what “role” you are expected to fulfill and how a normal game of Seton’s is won or lost. This is what we’ll discuss here.

## Some Observations

The map is mostly water.
There is a way to attack via land, the causeway, but this is through an easily defensible choke point and your army is vulnerable to navy bombardment along the way.
From the map layout alone, land pushes are mostly doomed to fail.
The map is almost, but not quite, mirrored. This is because the Seton’s map everyone plays (filename “scmp_009”) was shipped with the original Supreme Commander and was created by GPG before map mirroring tools were created. There is also a “FAF version” of Seton's that is properly mirrored but almost nobody plays it. Even tournaments use the original, non-mirrored map. The simple reason for that is that the original map got grandfathered in.
There are no underwater mexes anywhere. This is one of the many reasons why T1 subs are generally quite bad on Seton’s. (See below for a discussion on subs as a noob trap.)
In fact, at the start of the game there is effectively nothing to fight for inside any of the ponds. Navy is only threatening when it reaches the water’s edge where it can start to bombard bases.
The Air player is completely isolated from all land and navy aggression until at least one of their allies dies first. So for most of the game, the Air player can only directly be threatened via air. This of course includes everything the enemy Air player does, but also t1/t2 bombers and/or drops by any of the other players.
Similarly, the Air player can only really contribute to the game by making air. The map is too large and their spawn is too far back for anything else to be viable. Due to this isolation and the size of the map the air player’s only viable (non-cheese) strategy is to skip T1 and T2 air entirely and rush T3 air as fast as possible, so that they can actually contribute to the game.
The mexes are very spread out. This makes T2/T3 air and drops very strong, as stationary AA can only protect a small part of your total eco.

## A Normal Game of Seton’s

Even though I describe a “normal” game of Seton’s here, only a minority of your Seton’s games will actually be “normal”. Weird stuff happens all the time.
Still, understanding how a default Seton’s game flows is helpful as a reference and, everything else being equal, it should inform your default strategy as well.

Note that you are not forced to follow the playbook I outline below. What I describe here is just the simplest way to play Seton’s.

## The First 10 Minutes

The Mid player Rushes and fights for the causeway wrecks, and stops their land production after that reclaim is gone and they feel safe from further land aggression, ecoing up afterwards.
The Rock player Rushes a transport to claim their island asap, and enters navy after that.
The Beach player Just ecos up a bit and/or assists the mid player in getting the causeway reclaim and/or makes some early T1 bombers for harassment. They then enter navy at roughly the same time as the Rock player.
The Air player Sim city’s their way to T3 air asap, completely skipping T1 and T2 air.

## Minute 10 - 20

Mid Enters navy in one of the ponds, usually on the side of their Beach. They keep an eye on the enemy Mid player to make sure they don’t get overwhelmed by a surprise T3/T4 land push, but other than that no more land units are made. The focus is entirely on navy.
Rock and Beach Either fight for the pond on T2 navy or just harass each other while further ecoing.
The Air Player scales up further, protecting their allies from drops, bombers and torps.

## Minute 20 - 30

All players Are on full T3 or post-T3 eco.
Both ponds Are engaged in a T3 navy battle.
The Air players

Do their best to assist whichever pond they think has the best chances of winning, usually the Rock pond. If a decisive air fight happens, the navy players need to quickly make some cruisers to avoid getting torped into oblivion.

## Minute 30 - 40

Clear winners emerge in each of the ponds.
If both ponds are won by the same team, they have won the game.
If not, the race is on to kill the remaining enemy slots faster than they kill yours. Usually this is done via some combination of a land invasion, experimentals, nuke subs and game enders.

## Don’t Take This Playbook As Gospel

Lower rated Seton’s (all lower rated games really) are very much a clusterfuck. Strange stuff will happen all the time. This is fine. Just be flexible so that you can react to weird stuff appropriately. And don’t be a dick that blames their allies for not doing “their job”. That’s neither helpful during nor after the game.
