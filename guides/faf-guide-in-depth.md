# The FAF guide: basic concepts in depth

[The original](https://docs.google.com/document/d/13S4nBDfcBK4WmFtykXGKNmvIPe9L2nbiriISpHNgE4U/edit).

This guide is primarily centered at assisting new players, particularly new players that are competitively inclined and wish to know how to understand the interrelations between the fundamental resources in FAF. These relations between mass, energy, and buildpower dictate the viability of all builds, tactics, and strategies in the game. If you want to begin critically analyzing your plays as well as the plays of others, understanding the mechanisms between these resources is imperative. If you are a new player dumping his resources into inefficient sources, it is extremely easy to pinpoint and change this behavior. If you are a new player that doesn’t know how to gather his resources or even what they are and what they mean, then it’s going to be very hard to understand why certain strategies or moves are bad in the language of the game. Glossary: Before getting into anything, it’s important to understand some terminology about the game. These terms will be used during the guide, will be used by people in the game, and will likely be expected knowledge of you in any competitive sphere of FAF. Mex - Mass Extractor, the green squares on a map (see picture)

Hydro - Hydrocarbon Plant, the yellow square on a map (see picture)

Pgens - Power Generators ACU - Armored Command Unit, the first unit you have in the match (see picture) BP - buildpower, this refers to anything that has the ability to build

Mass - the green resource bar in the top left Energy/e - the yellow resource bar in the top left

BO - build order, essentially a memorized production formula to apply to a map Float - Excess resources within your mass or energy storage

Overflow - Having your mass or energy storage filled and continuing to float mass or energy

Stall - running a negative (or red) expenditure of a resource while having 0 in storage

Ring - to surround the structure; for a mex this typically refers to making 4 mass storages around it Engy/Engie - Engineer

## Chapter 1.1: Approaching the Game

When a new player expresses interest about playing FAF in a competitive manner, the first advice (or question) they nearly always receive is “Do you know the basic BOs?” Now, this isn’t necessarily bad advice, in fact it is actually very helpful for new players. The basic BOs teach you how to get to minute 4 or 5 in a game without completely losing control of your resources which in turn gives you a solid basis to improve from. However, the attractive laziness of this advice to both the person giving the advice as well as the person getting it results in predisposing new players to an awful approach to the game. These basic BOs are essentially just rote memorization. People learn, for example: factory, 4 mex, hydro, land factory, 1 pgen, land factory for a basic triple land opening on a map with a viable early hydro rush. Likewise, you can learn: factory, 2 pgen, 2 mex, 1 pgen, 2 mex, 5 pgen, air fac for a second air build with no nearby hydro. In addition, you learn minutiae such that you need 10 pgens instead of 8 if you plan on rushing a transport from second air rather than interceptors. For a full explanation, please watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_6uE1-xS2uk None of this information is bad. In fact it is a necessity to learn this information in order to properly keep up in the first few minutes on any map so that you have all the same tools as your opponent to expand and attack. If you do not know how to get interceptors faster than transports, then you will fail to punish transport rushes on 10x10s. Likewise, if you make land factories too late due to too little e or too much e production; you can lose a critical engagement on your half of the map control where you should have had more tanks. This can snowball immensely in the early game where your mirror was able to get 4 mexes up a minute faster than you resulting in a nearly 500 mass difference between you two. This 500 mass difference means that the opponent was able to get an additional 10 tanks to snowball into even more of an advantage and it was all caused by having 3 tanks against your 1 tank early on. All of this BO information can also be applied to nearly any map so it should be very easy to put the memorization into practice and in turn make it muscle memory.

I’d also recommend getting familiar with some UI mods that FAF offers. Some of them can assist you by providing you more information. Check this thread for more info:
https://forums.faforever.com/viewtopic.php?f=41&t=19207

Some Personal Recommendations: Common Mod Tools + Additional Camera Stuff (allows you to see build range of selected units around your cursor, helps with planning out engie paths and basebuilding) Supreme Economy (can give you a good glimpse of where exactly you are putting resources) Better Reclaim view (makes larger reclaim easier to see, tells you total mass on screen) Supreme Scoreboard (essentially required for analyzing replays)

## Chapter 1.2: The Phases

So after reading the past section, you might assume that the best way forward in improving is to learn precise, optimized ratios for how to do anything in FAF. For example, how many pgens you need to go t2 land. How many land factories you can support with the mexes you have. How many engies of bp a factory has. However, ironically, none of this knowledge is really going to improve your play.

FAF is divided into 2 sections of play. There is the Memorization Phase built on getting you into a position to interact with your opponent successfully on any map which is what we just described.

## HOWEVER

Once you have set up your initial production, you have left the Memorization Phase and have now entered the Interaction Phase.

DO NOT apply memorization to the Interaction Phase. This means NO questions like: “When should I reach full t3 eco?” “When should I get my first t2 mex?” “When do I go t2 land on Open Palms?” “How many engineers should I make?” “Do I make some mongoose or pillars?” “What’s a good power income for minute 10?”

In the Interaction Phase, your play is reactive. That means that what you should be doing is looking at the game situation and in particular, looking at what your opponent is trying to do in order to see if doing something like upgrading a mex is a good idea. The Interaction Phase is built on, well, interaction. That means there is no definite answer to questions such as these as they are fundamentally built on tactics rather than any formula.

## Chapter 2: The Three Resources

To get you into the mindset to understand how to approach the Interaction Phase of the game, we need to go back to the basic BOs that you can hopefully recall at will now. This section will be focused on approaching these BOs through the lens of understanding them through efficiency rather than simple memorization.

But what is efficiency in FAF? Well, it comes down to managing 3 resources. Yes 3, not 2. Your resources are:

- Mass
- Energy
- Buildpower

The first two should be evident and obvious for you to keep track of. You have a mass bar and you can very easily see if you are losing mass per second, increasing mass per second, are floating mass, are stalling mass, or are overflowing mass. The same is true for energy. Buildpower however, is a bit more difficult and more of a thing you simply need to gauge and predict from the state of your mass and energy bars.

The second insight should tell you that an intuitive truth is also the real truth. You want to ring your t2 mexes with storages before upgrading to t3 mexes.

## Chapter 3.2: Reclaim

Many players may wonder, if map control is so important and so efficient at allowing you to scale, why is it that there are players that exist on the ladder that can turtle on 4 or 6 mexes, tech them up, and easily win against players that got 75-80% map control? The answer is simple: reclaim.

When you first load up into a map you aren’t familiar with, the first thing you should do after queueing your factory is zooming out to look at the mexes as well as to ctrl+shift and see where the reclaim concentrations are on the map. For mexes, you want to make mental notes of the most efficient ways to grab mexes through engies. Are you going to need to make transports because the mexes are far away? Are you going to need to send only a few engies out to expand? What engies are going to need the most protection? Where will you send your ACU?

When it comes to reclaim, you want to take note of any high concentrations of reclaim near areas of contention like the middle of the map. These are going to be high priority areas where you will likely want to rush to in order to control. Just as importantly, you want to take note of reclaim near your base that you can incorporate into your build. Do you have a lot of trees nearby? You might be able to get away with less pgens, just watch your energy bar. Do you have a lot of rocks? Maybe you could use more engies in your base to help build energy faster so you can scale faster. Then finally, you can try to pick up reclaim with your engies as they go out to expand to mex locations. No point in sending two engies to do the job of one, especially early on.

When it comes to mass there are 2 central types of reclaim to keep into account and another that is more minor. The two central types are wrecks (blue) and natural reclaim (red).

Natural reclaim in regards to mass mainly refers to rocks, however, you should also be aware that tree groups do carry some mass in them as do civilian buildings found throughout many FAF maps. Here you can see a wide range of mass reclaim found in rocks. You can see this yourself by pressing ctrl+shift in game. However, due to current limitations in the game code, you cannot see rocks that have a mass value lower than 10. Unfortunately in some maps, particularly desert maps, there exist rocks that are not picked up by ctrl+shift due to that minimum value limitation, but they still provide resources for you. These rocks in the screenshot above provide 7 mass each. Currently, the only efficient way to learn about these is through analyzing replays of people that beat you by using this reclaim. Here you can see that my mass reclaimed has gone up, even though I’ve only reclaimed an area of tree groups. Here you can see civilian trucks and pgens that are not currently dead, while they do not show any mass value, they do in fact carry mass within them. After being attacked by bombers, you can see they carry quite a significant amount of mass within them. A lot of maps will simply have civilian structures already destroyed so you can immediately see their value through ctrl+shift.

In addition, you should note that the reclaim values between the 2 identical t2 pgens are different. Why is that? It’s because wrecks themselves actually have health. The wreck of a unit, at the full 81% mass value, has 90% of the hp that the unit would have if it was alive. This means that the t2 pgen wrecks above both had 2250 hp. When I killed them, the explosion of the second pgen (red) harmed the reclaim of the first pgen (blue) which in turn caused the mass value to go down proportional to the damage it caused.

Wrecks are considered the reclaim that pops up as a result of battle or some form of destruction. You can see this in the result of a battle between two large groups of units Here you can see the result of a failed push. The person that successfully defended will now be able to invest the mass from this attack back into either his economy or units, depending on the game state. This is why failed attacks really hurt. Not only did you lose the mass you invested into the attack, but the enemy in return gets up to 81% of the mass you put into those units to then feed back into his war machine.

If you haven’t caught on, that is precisely what causes turtles to often defeat more aggressive map-control focused players. The map-control players presume that their more efficient play means they should be able to quickly overrun the enemy if they just dump their economy into units. However, the enemy that is turtling could have made mass efficient counters to the enemy units and so the 3k mass into tanks attacking the 1.5k mass in pd has now left potentially 2k mass in reclaim for the turtle player to now creep forward and take further map control. If the cycle continues, it is very easy to turn what should have been a huge macro advantage for the aggressive player into a game loss. Long story short: Do not become an organic mex for a turtle player. If you have the map control, you can scale up more efficiently than the player that has less map control. It is his responsibility to push into you to stay relevant to the game. If he does not, then you can win in 8 minutes when you have double, triple, quadruple his mass income. At that point, it doesn’t matter what you throw at him, you will win.

Another important aspect of reclaim is that you should look at reclaim like funds in a checking account accruing 0% interest. You want to immediately get these funds (reclaim) and utilize them to jumpstart some sort of safe investment like mex upgrades or some sort of entrepreneurial venture like making an extra 10 tanks to gain more map control from your opponent. The longer you leave reclaim sitting there doing nothing, the less value you will get from those funds as your ventures and investments continue to grow while your funds in your checking account do not. This is why you will often see that the most efficient build orders on a map are those that utilize reclaim as quickly as possible in order to jump start the scaling process as fast as possible.

## Chapter 4: Energy

Ah the energy bar, the red-headed stepchild of new players on FAF. The idea that energy is a resource you should be taking care of in order to minimize resource waste is often alien to players until they get to the 1000-1300 rating on FAF. They see it as a resource bar to keep full and as long as it’s full, you’re doing a good job.

Like many things, this mindset is not exactly wrong. Having slight overflow of power is nowhere near as terrible as overflowing mass as power is a resource highly in flux. You might want some extra generation for overcharge, for a sudden build project heavy in e cost like an ACU upgrade, or just because you don’t feel comfortable dancing on the edge with the terrible outcome of an e stall.

Why is an e stall bad? Well because it combines the terribleness of overflowing mass with the terribleness of not having any e. Your mexes require e to run, and so having a massive power stall will result in your mexes failing to run which in turn reduces your mass income which is effectively resource waste similar to overflowing mass. Your buildings will stall in production due to not having the necessary energy income to complete all your production demands and they will also possibly take longer due to stalling mass. You should try to avoid an e stall the same way you try to avoid mass overflow. If you do not, you could get the fabled mass AND energy stall where both your resources bar blink red to showcase how bad of a situation you have just dug yourself into.

However the outlook of just pretending energy overflow is alright does not help us understand the game, it’s simply training wheels. If we want to understand the game, we need to take those wheels off even if it may hurt us in the process.

## Chapter 4.1: Power Generators and Hydros

Power generators are your central source of consistent power income in this game. Similar to mexes, there are 3 tiers all with varying efficiency. Additionally, there are also hydrocarbons. In order to understand the ecopath and tactics people use for scaling energy, let’s look at some statistics once again.

Mass Cost      T1 Pgen  T2 Pgen  T3 Pgen  Hydro 75       1200     3240     160 Power Income   20       500      2500     100 3.75     2.4      1.296    1.6 Mass Cost per Unit of Power

This one is a fair bit easier to understand than the mex chart. There is no need to worry about e storage efficiency in the same way mass storages need to be considered. Generally, e storages should be kept away from power locations due to the fact they explode if they die causing damage to buildings around them. Power generators often have little hp and so that explosive damage could cause a t2 pgen to die to a single t2 bomber pass rather than two. It isn’t worth the risk for the potential 10 or 80 extra power it gives.

So essentially, the higher you move up in tiers for pgens the more efficient they become. The only exception is the hydro which is only slightly less efficient than a t3 pgen. This is why you never see high rated players reclaim their hydros once they reach t3 power but you will see them reclaim t1 pgens and t2 pgens. People reclaim their lower tier power when they no longer need it as that mass can be recycled into higher tier pgens that are more efficient and then the excess mass can be turned into additional units.

This does not mean that you should immediately recycle t1 pgens once you get to t2 pgens however. You should see whether you will need the energy from those t1 pgens now for something such as a quick ACU upgrade or additional air production or not. It ultimately comes down to how the game state looks.

Getting back to the efficiency of the hydro, you should now be able to understand why it is so critical to get a hydro rush build order for the maps where you have a hydro near your spawn. You are able to get 100 power for 1.6 mass per unit of power. That’s 160 mass used. To do the same with t1 pgens you would need to use 375 mass. That’s over a 200 mass difference which equates to having the ability to make an extra 4 tanks or an extra 2 pgens for something like a faster 2nd air timing. Always build hydros.

## Chapter 4.2: Trees & Tree groups

Unfortunately trees and tree groups are not as intuitively shown as reclaim within the client. You will not be able to see energy through the ctrl+shift reclaim view and so instead this is something that can only be learned through game experience. The first thing we should do is explain the concept of tree groups. In FAF, there are two possible types of trees: singular props and tree groups. A singular prop is just what it sounds, a single tree itself. These are essentially never worth focusing on and are often included in reclaim piles as a way of slowing down reclaim and rewarding manual reclaim.

Here you can see a fusion of dead singular trees mixed in with rocks, these trees themselves provide nearly no resources and can be ignored in the context of energy reclaim. However, these trees are not singular trees but rather giant groups which the game treats as a singular prop unless the prop is broken through some sort of physical interaction such as a unit moving through it or a fire burning the trees.

You can very faintly see a change in the color of trees that have been interacted with (red). These trees are no longer the singular tree group entity, but rather a large collection of tree props now. Does this mean that these broken trees are now as worthless as those singular trees mentioned above? NO! It is not the end of the world if you break your tree groups. They can be very important to micromanage in the early game when it is hugely impactful to get the extra e as quickly as possible to, for example, to skip on 1 or 2 pgens for your first air factory. You can see this on Loki when people send their early engy to reclaim tree groups near the spawn.

However, FAF has a built in mechanism that makes you gain extra resources from broken tree groups to make up for the extra time it takes to reclaim them. In the long term, this mechanism prevents people from falling behind due to broken tree groups. You have bigger things to worry about than destroying them, so just send some engies into a forest with an attack move to clear out the trees and get the energy one way or another.

## Chapter 4.3: Getting Both Resources

There is a final way to gather resources from reclaim: building on top of reclaim. This is much more of a rarity than the other forms covered here, but it’s still a good thing to keep in mind for the occasional situations where it could come in handy. Let’s look at a hypothetical game scenario. Imagine you are a player that has just built 2 t2 pgens. A couple corsairs come and snipe both your pgens, causing you to have a massive power stall. How can you recover as quickly as possible?

The easiest way would be to start building BOTH t2 pgens, but then proceeding to reclaim one while continuing to rebuild the other. This is because a building that IS reclaim provides you 81% of its mass assuming it is an undamaged wreck, but it provides you 0% of the power. An unfinished building that is then reclaimed will provide you a refund of the mass and energy that you used to build that building. Since a rebuilt wreckage spawns in half-built, this means you could get both power AND mass which means you are able to quickly climb over that powerstall to at least get some of your resources back online.

## Chapter 5: Buildpower

Buildpower is a big term that covers a lot of units. What does it mean? Basically, “Unit that consumes a set amount of mass and energy for some set or infinite period.”

The most obvious form of buildpower is engies. They can not only be used to build and assist things, but they are also your form of buildpower that can be used to interact with the game and GIVE you resources. But buildpower also can refer to many other structures. Some of the other commonly used forms of buildpower are: the ACU, mexes themselves, factories, and radar.

These all have varying resource expenditures and it will be up to you as a player to learn what takes what resources. Some good general guidelines are: Land factories take a fair bit of mass income and little e.

Air factories take little mass but a lot of e.

Navy factories take a lot of mass and a lot of e.

ACU upgrades take a fair bit of mass and a lot of e

Mexes take a lot of mass and a fair bit of e If you notice your mass bar is filling up but you don’t exactly have much e, then you certainly shouldn’t decide to invest buildpower into air. Instead, you should devote it into pgens or land units. Basically, you should use your mass and energy bars as a diagnostic tool to gauge the health of your current game state. They should be what dictate the placement of your build capacity.

If you begin feeling adventurous and actually want to plan to do some sort of tactic like a corsair snipe or a t3 land rush, then you have to respond to the dictations of your diagnostic tools as you go for that tactic. As you play more and more games, eventually these tactics themselves will become muscle memory and you will be able to feel when you have “enough resources” in order to successfully manage a tactic. Some people can just spawn in and gauge what they need to get in order to make a tactic work out!

## Chapter 6: Interactions between the Resources

So what does all of this mean for you? Basically, the point of these past few pages was to explain where to get continuous mass and energy income, mexes and pgens, and the aspects that allow you to convert buildpower into mass and energy, reclaim and trees.

There are 4 Bad States for your Mass and Energy management, I will list them in order of severity:

- Energy stall (solved by: trees, pausing production other than pgens)
- Mass overflow (solved by: making bp, making pgens)
- Energy overflow = Mass stall (solved by: reclaim, using energy intensive bp)

If you are currently energy stalling, you are unfortunately in a very bad situation. An energy stall often means you have made an error a minute or more in the past and most solutions are going to require you to bite the bullet and just struggle through the inefficiency. Pgens themselves cost quite a lot of power, a t1 pgen costs 750 e for 20 continuous income. This means that if you see yourself stalling a fair bit of power, your natural inclination of putting more engies on power production will inevitably cause you to go deeper in the hole before it can actually cause you to get out of it.

The only “quick” solutions to the dilemma are either putting more buildpower on tree reclaim to subsidize your expenditures or to take buildpower off of high energy expenditures. This could mean stopping a mex upgrade, stopping an ACU upgrade, or taking some engies off of an air factory. Be careful about solving your energy crisis through nonrenewable resources though, a common cause of energy stalls is the loss of trees without the creation of pgens to make up for the loss!

Mass overflow is a lot easier to resolve. You can take buildpower off of reclaim and instead use it to make land factories (which are more buildpower) and then put the engies back on reclaim later on once the issue is resolved. Another potential solution is to utilize mexes as emergency buildpower by upgrading them to a higher tier. Yet be aware, this is a temporary solution and if you do not increase your buildpower, you will end up with even more mass and find yourself back at square 1.

When it comes to mass stalls, you should simply take buildpower off of, well, building things and instead utilize it for reclaim and trees. A small stall is inconsequential and can actually be efficient as it means that any reclaim will be utilized by your production as soon as it comes in.

When it comes to energy overflow, you should simply put extra buildpower on high energy production. For example, an often done tactic is to do an ACU upgrade once your first t2 pgen is up and you get a sudden boost of +500 energy with little buildpower around to properly utilize it in things like air production. You could also make a few energy storages so that you run less of a risk of stalling once you get the overflow under control, and you can also then utilize the overflow for damage through the overcharge mechanic!

Final Comments You should now have a solid understanding of what it is that often decides FAF games and what separates the good from the bad. You should be getting your map half as quickly as possible with your buildpower while preventing your opponent from getting his. You should be utilizing the reclaim on your half as soon as possible in order to boost your ability to scale. Finally, you should utilize tactics that synergize well with the resources available to you on the map which you can figure out by looking at your resource bars.

You should now have the tools to be able to critically analyze your games and notice at least several major errors in your play. Believe me, these flaws do not disappear. If you are able to recognize and then fix these errors in preceding games, you will have a much easier time increasing your competency as a player.
