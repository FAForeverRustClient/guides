# Seton's: the novice guide

By CheeseBerry.

In this section we’ll go over some more general observations, typical strategies, noob traps, and conclusions we can draw from all of those which are applicable to basically every game you play.

## Reclaim Distribution

In total there is about 50k total reclaim on the map, “only” 12k of which is in the wrecks on the causeway. Tree reclaim is important, kids! The only slot that can more or less ignore tree reclaim is Mid, as they have the more important center reclaim to fight over. All other slots should put at least some effort into collecting their trees in a timely manner. For tree reclaim alone, Beach and Rock want at least one factory to make 10-15 reclaim engies, Air wants two factories making 20-30 reclaim engies. Rule of thumb: Stop making additional

engies once half the reclaim is gone. Btw. this heuristic works for other types of spread out reclaim on other maps too.
Also note that trees not only give mass, but also energy, so the ~7k mass in trees that e.g. the air player collects will also give them 70k energy too.
The couple rocks on the map matter quite a lot for the Rock player as it is very close to their spawn point. Any good Rock opening must make use of them. The rocks in the Air player’s section are just a nice bonus, but of course you shouldn’t forget about them either.

## Matchups

Rock vs Beach AND Mid on navy is a roughly even matchup. While it is a 1v2 in player count, and it is 18 mexes vs 23, the Mid player will be delayed in their eco a bit due to the early fighting for the reclaim, so the total mass available for navy production is about equal on both sides.

## Where to Fight

As mentioned earlier, there is nothing to fight for in the ponds themselves. Only their edges matter. As such, you want to fight as close as possible to your side of the map, but not so close that your eco, base or harbor are actually in danger.
During the T1 stage, you want to fight basically right in front of, or maybe even inside of your harbor.

## Stalling the Enemy

In close games, your team will usually win the navy fight for one of the ponds and lose the fight for the other one. This means that often, you’ll be on the losing pond’s side.
Yet, because bases are so spread out on Seton’s, losing the battle for your pond, doesn’t mean that the game is lost. If you stall well enough, your team can often win the game on the other side of the map, while you prevent your enemies from making progress.
You want to be as annoying as possible to your enemies, to prevent them from winning for as long as possible. Be the best road block you can.
Reclaim, scout, make preparations to defend against a land invasion, get com upgrades and micro your heart out at the front lines of the other pond.
If you think the game is truly over for your entire team, vote on it using the recall button.
If your team votes against recall, but you still want to leave, gift your base to an ally and use your ACU to go out in a blaze of glory at the front lines.

## Seton’s Noob Traps

Noob traps are strategies that are only strong in lower rated lobbies and that get weaker the better your enemies become.
There are quite a few noob traps in the game. Here we will cover the ones most common on Seton’s.

## Not Enough Eco

Seton’s is a defensive map and the vast majority of games go into the T3 stage and beyond.
As such, keeping pace on eco with your enemies is even more important on Seton’s than on most other maps. If you don’t, you’ll just become irrelevant at some point. No amount of micro or clever tactics will make you win against an enemy that has twice your eco and army size.
Very roughly, a T3 mex takes 5 minutes to pay itself back, post-T3 eco (mass fabs, bois) need about 8 minutes.

As a general guideline, if you expect the game to go on for at least 10 more minutes, it is in a mostly stable gamestate, and your team is not currently working on a game ender, you should probably eco more.
(T1) Submarines
This section is mostly Seton’s specific advice. While this the same reasoning also largely applies to any other navy centric map without underwater mexes, submarines are particularly bad on setons due to the high eco, long navy travel times, and easily defended shore lines.
Subs, especially higher tech ones, do sometimes have legitimate uses. If you are starting out though I’d heavily recommend just not making any on maps without underwater mexes due to the reasons outlined below.

## The Strength of T1 Subs

Subs are hard to scout and are literally unkillable by most units even if they are found.
Why T1 Subs are Bad (on Seton’s)
Their stats suck, they have no utility and they can’t threaten anything.  Compare the stats of a T1 sub with a frig: T1 Subs cost 25% more, deal 25% less damage, and have less than one third the health!
Subs have no radar, they can’t attack into an enemy harbor as a single T1 torp defense counters them super hard and they can’t even kill exposed mexes near the water without losing their only real strength by surfacing.
They are very slow at killing engies (always some of the juiciest targets) and can’t even target aeon/sera engies without surfacing due to them being hover units.
Notably, subs and frigs both have the same speed. If your enemy retreats their frigs, or even just moves them around so that your subs can’t catch up to them, there is nothing your T1 subs can do during the T1 stage.
Worse than that though, they are even less useful later on as during the T2/T3 stage torpedo bombers and destroyers eat them for breakfast.

## Why T1 Subs Feel Strong in Low Rated Games

Lower rated players don’t know how to deal with subs and due to this knowledge gap they may as well be truly unkillable. Often their only answer is to spam subs themselves or, if they don’t do that, they just lose their entire navy instead.
Often they don’t even realize that their units are being killed at all, so even if they know how to counter them, they realize far too late that they need to.
Submarines being strong against weak players, but weak against strong players, is why they are a noob trap.

## Countering T1 subs

To be fair, knowing how to counter T1 subs isn’t the most intuitive thing, hence them being effective against inexperienced players at all.
The only real strength T1 subs have is that they are untargetable by most units. Basically everything that can target them, completely destroys them mass for mass.
As such, the main counters to subs are torp launchers, torp bombers and destroyers.

## Torpedo Launchers

T1 Torpedo Launchers/Defenses build about three times as fast as a submarine (~500 vs ~1500 build time), have significantly more range (~50 vs ~30) and trade with them about 2:1 in mass.
This is without even taking into account that you can build them behind your naval yards, effectively quadrupling their HP and other micro tricks you can use to make defense against subs even easier.  If your enemy is spamming subs during the T1 stage, you can literally just make 2 torp launchers behind your naval yard, and never fear dying to subs. Even better, you can just have a couple engies chilling next to your naval yard and make the torp launchers only when needed, because they build so fast.
T1 submarines are effectively incapable of ever threatening your naval production due to this.

## Torpedo Bombers

Torpedo bombers are, arguably, the best navy unit in the game. They might not seem that way at first but for the same reason that subs are bad torpedo bombers are straight up amazing:

Their stats are ridiculously good. They are super cheap, about the cost of a frig and deal an absurd 600 damage per drop.
In fact, torpedo bombers two-hit all T1 subs, even though the submarine costs significantly more than a torp bomber. The submarine can’t even fight back.
You could argue that this is similar to how a submarine “counters” a frigate: The frig can’t fight back either, afterall. The difference is that a torpedo bomber takes two passes, about 10 seconds to kill a sub, while a sub takes an entire minute to kill a single frig. A minute is an eternity!
Furthermore a frig can just retreat and the sub can’t catch up as they have the same speed, while the torpedo bomber is much faster than a sub.
In fact, torpedo bombers are a very valuable support unit to basically any navy composition, even if your enemy isn’t building any subs at all. If they are, it’s just gravy because you can make a couple additional torp bombers and get value for free.

## Torpedo Bombers - Spread Attack

For torpedo bombers to work well against sub spam though, you need to give them the right orders, or they will be much less effective. (Yet another knowledge gap making subs strong against inexperienced players.)
The problem is that, if you just give an attack or move command in the general direction of where the subs are, the torps will kill them, but they will do so quite inefficiently as they’ll hugely overkill one or two subs at a time.
The secret to avoid this is the “Distribute Orders” hotkey (in its previous incarnation called “Spread Attack”). As the name suggests, it’s a way to distribute multiple orders evenly among your units. For our purposes, it allows us to give attack orders to submarines and then distribute them among our torp bombers, so that each t1 sub is targeted by two torp bombers each. This will result in every sub dying in a single pass, after which we can simply retreat our torp bombers to do the same later.
Spread Attacking torpedo bombers is one of the rare situations in FAF where micro can greatly increase a unit’s usefulness.
Go into the keybind menu, bind “Distribute Orders” and/or its shift version to an easily reachable hotkey (I have it on shift-Z for example), and use it whenever the opportunity presents itself. On Seton’s that will mostly be torp bombers, but there are plenty of other common uses too. It even works with move and build commands, or any combination of them.

## Destroyers

All non-UEF destroyers trade very mass efficiently against T1 subs, without even using any of their surface guns. UEF destroyers are the exception, as they have no relevant torpedo weapons. UEF needs torpedo boats instead or to just stick with torp bombers against subs.  To fight subs of any tech level with destros, just drive your destros on top of them.
Destroyers trading so well against T1 subs is another reason why they are bad as they effectively “die for free” during the T2 navy stage, even if your opponent didn’t make any torps at all.
So when should you make T1 subs?
T1 subs do have legitimate uses, sometimes even on Seton’s.
First of, and most common, is to build submarines on maps with underwater mexes, both to defend your own and to deny your opponents. This only makes sense before T2 air or navy is

on the field, as destros and torp bombers do that job much better, but before that only T1 subs can do this job.
A very small number of T1 subs can also be used to harass enemy frigs away from strategically important areas. This is sometimes useful even on Seton’s to deny the enemy frig-radar coverage to an area without having to invest into enough frigs yourself to force them away via numbers alone. Even though this is a very niche use case, it’s technically the most common reason for T1 subs to be built in high rated Seton’s. Please don’t do this. The benefit is negligible and the downside is that, as mentioned, T1 subs just die for free during the T2 stage of navy. Just make like 2 frigs instead and you can achieve the same with units that are actually useful later.
T1 Sub’s can also be used to attack low health underwater ACUs during the early game. On Seton’s this is only rarely relevant, but it does happen that a commander needs to retreat from the causeway through water and in this spot only T1 subs can finish it off. Until you are at least 1.5k (and know what you are doing) this situation is the only time on Seton’s where you are legally allowed to make T1 subs.
What about T2/T3 subs?
Cybran and Aeon have T2 subs, Sera even has a T3 sub. These are much better along every dimension compared to T1 subs, and even then they are only occasionally useful.
This is because even though they are way better than T1 subs, they still only trade about mass evenly with Sera and Cybran destros. UEF torp boats, Aeon destros and of course torp bombers still absolutely murder them.
Yet, T2 subs can legitimately be strong if you have air control and your enemy underbuild destros.
T2 subs can also shut down a UEF Battlecruiser rush that doesn’t have air or torp boat backup, as BCs don’t have any torpedo weapons either. The same is mostly true for T3 subs, but they are legitimately strong in large late-game T3 navy fights. Due to the recent torpedo mechanics rework, groups of them don’t overkill enemy ships nearly as much anymore, while themselves being harder to kill with torpedo bombers. (Arguably, they are currently too strong even. They’ll probably get nerfed in the future.)
Still, T2/T3 subs have the big problem of just evaporating if you have misjudged the situation, or if your Air isn’t paying attention for like 30 seconds. Use them as counters for the specific situations they are good in, but keep in mind that overbuilding them can lead to a very quick game loss.

How To Counter T1 Sub Spam on Seton’s Step by Step
If they surprise attack you, first and foremost move your navy away from them.
They have the same speed as your normal T1 navy, so you can just move away and they’ll never catch you. Also, there is no reason to fight them, as they can’t harm anything but your navy or your facs. They can’t catch your navy, and if they move near your naval facs, you just build torp launchers there, which kill them easily.
If you are going for a T2 navy rush (as non-uef), your destroyers will clean them up for free, so you can just wait for those.
If you don’t, make a T2 air HQ. You want to build one in basically any Seton’s game at some point anyway, as torpedo bombers are an essential part of any mid- and late-game navy. Then make a couple torps and send them to kill your opponents subs once they attack by using the “Distribute Orders” command.

## Summary - Novice Guide

There is almost 40k mass in trees on the map. Reclaim them.
You don’t have to fight navy on their side of the pond. When you are on defense, retreating further back is often a good idea.
Stalling the enemy on one side of the map so that your allies can win the game on the other side is a very important skill on Seton’s.
If you expect the game to go on for at least 10 more minutes you should probably eco more.
T1 subs are bad on Seton’s because their stats are bad and they can’t threaten anything of value. Kill them with torpedo defenses, torpedo bombers, destros and/or torp boats.
Torpedo bombers are much stronger when using the “Distribute Orders” hotkey with them.
